PREAMBLE

We, the members of TINUBU SOLIDARITY INITIATIVE, united by our shared belief in democracy, justice, and progress, do hereby establish this Constitution as the supreme guiding document of our organization.

Recognizing the importance of civic participation in governance in Nigeria, we commit ourselves to mobilizing citizens, promoting transparency, and fostering unity across ethnic, religious, and social divides.

We affirm our dedication to empowering youth and women, advancing community development, and supporting leaders who embody integrity and accountability.

In pursuit of these ideals, we pledge to uphold the rule of law, respect human rights, and work peacefully to strengthen democratic institutions.

This Constitution shall serve as the foundation of our collective efforts, ensuring that our actions remain guided by fairness, inclusivity, and the unwavering commitment to the progress of our nation.

ARTICLE 1

NAME, STATUS, HEADQUARTERS, AND IDENTITY

1. NAME OF THE INITIATIVE

The Association shall be known as TINUBU SOLIDARITY INITIATIVE (hereinafter referred to as "THE ASSOCIATION").

2. LEGAL STATUS

The Association shall operate as a voluntary, non-governmental and non-profit/non-political support Association in Nigeria, in accordance with the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

3. HEADQUARTERS

The National Headquarters of the Association shall be situate in Nigeria. The Association may establish State, Local Government, Ward, and Community Chapters across the Country and in the diaspora as deemed necessary.

4. MOTTO

The motto of the Association shall be IN PURSUIT OF THE RENEWED HOPE AGENDA, GOOD GOVERNANCE TOWARDS NIGERIA NATIONHOOD AND SURVIVAL reflecting its vision and mission.

5. EMBLEM/LOGO

The Association shall adopt an official emblem/logo, which shall be used on all official documents, banners, and communications. The emblem shall symbolize unity, progress, and democratic values.

6. GUIDING PRINCIPLES

The Association shall be guided by the principles of:

  • Democracy and respect for the rule of law.
  • Inclusivity and equal participation of all members regardless of gender, ethnicity, or religion.
  • Transparency and accountability in leadership and governance.
  • Peaceful mobilization and civic education.
AIMS AND OBJECTIVES

AIMS AND OBJECTIVES OF TINUBU SOLIDARITY INITIATIVE

  1. To educate members and the wider community on their civic rights and responsibilities.
  2. To share best democratic practices and strengthen democratic culture.
  3. To encourage and promote peaceful participation in democratic processes.
  4. To promote awareness of the Nigerian Constitution, electoral laws, and democratic institutions.
  5. To advocate for good governance, transparency, and accountability in public office.
  6. To engage with policymakers and stakeholders on issues affecting citizens.
  7. To encourage active participation of youth and women in politics and leadership.
  8. To provide platforms for training, mentorship, and capacity building.
  9. To initiate and support community-based projects that improve social welfare.
  10. To partner with other NGOs, civil society, and government agencies for development initiatives.
  11. To foster unity among diverse ethnic, religious, and social groups.
  12. To ensure equal opportunity and representation for all members.
  13. To build relationships with other democratic support groups, both within and outside Nigeria.
  14. To mobilize grassroots support for chosen candidates, parties, or democratic causes.

ARTICLE 2

VISION, MISSION AND OBJECTIVES

1. VISION

The Vision of the Association shall be to promote civic engagement, democratic participation, and good governance in Nigeria.

2. MISSION

The Mission of the Association is as follows:

  • To mobilize citizens around towards shared democratic values and national development goals.
  • To promote political awareness and civic education.
  • To support leadership ideals aligned with national unity, economic development, and democratic consolidation.
  • To encourage lawful and peaceful participation in Nigeria's democratic processes.

3. OBJECTIVES

a. Political Mobilization: To mobilize grassroots support for democratic governance. To encourage peaceful Civic engagement.

b. Civic Education: To educate members and the public on civic rights and responsibilities.

c. Advocacy and Policy Engagement: To advocate for good governance, transparency, and accountability in public office.

d. Youth and Women Empowerment: To encourage active participation of youth and women in governance and leadership. To provide platforms for training, mentorship, and capacity building.

e. Community Development: To initiate and support community-based projects that improve social welfare. To partner with NGOs, civil society, and government agencies for development initiatives.

f. Unity and Inclusivity: To foster unity among diverse ethnic, religious, and social groups. To advocate for equal opportunity and representation for all members and stakeholders.

g. International Collaboration: To build relationships with other Associations both within and outside Nigeria. To share best practices and strengthen democratic culture and good governance.

ARTICLE 3

MEMBERSHIP

1. ELIGIBILITY

Membership shall be open to all Nigerian citizens aged 18 years and above.

2. CATEGORIES OF MEMBERSHIP

  • Founding Members: Individuals who engineered and birthed the existence of the Association.
  • Ordinary Members: Individuals who register and actively participate in the Association's activities.
  • Student Members: Young people in tertiary institutions who join through campus chapters.
  • Honorary Members: Distinguished individuals recognized for their contributions to democratic governance and Association's objectives.
  • Associate Members: Supporters who may not be able to participate actively but contribute financially or morally.

3. RIGHTS OF MEMBERS

  • To vote and be voted for in the Association elections (except honorary and associate members).
  • To participate in meetings, discussions, and decision-making processes.
  • To access information and benefit from training, mentorship, and empowerment programs.

4. DUTIES OF MEMBERS

  • To uphold and defend the constitution of the Association.
  • To pay membership dues and levies as determined by the Executive Committee.
  • To participate actively in mobilization, advocacy, and community development activities.
  • To maintain discipline, respect leadership, and promote unity within the Association.

5. MEMBERSHIP REGISTER

The Secretary-General shall maintain an up-to-date register of all members, including their category and status.

6. TERMINATION OF MEMBERSHIP

Membership may be terminated for: Gross misconduct or anti-democratic behaviour; Non-payment of dues for two consecutive years; Actions that bring the Association into disrepute. A member facing termination shall have the right to a fair hearing before the Disciplinary Committee.

ARTICLE 4

LEADERSHIP STRUCTURE

1. ORGANS OF LEADERSHIP

The leadership of the Association shall consist of: National Executive Committee (NEC), State Chapters, Local Government Chapters, and Ward/Community Chapters.

2. NATIONAL EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE (NEC)

The NEC shall be the highest decision-making body of the Association and shall comprise:

  • President/Coordinator: Provides overall leadership, represents the Association nationally and internationally, and presides over meetings.
  • Vice President: Assists the President and acts in their absence.
  • Secretary-General: Responsible for correspondence, records, and documentation.
  • Assistant Secretary: Supports the Secretary-General and handles delegated tasks.
  • Treasurer: Manages finances, keeps records of income and expenditure.
  • Financial Secretary: Collects dues, donations, and prepares financial reports.
  • Public Relations Officer (PRO): Handles publicity, media relations, and communication.
  • Organizing Secretary: Coordinates events, mobilization, and logistics.
  • Legal Adviser: Provides legal guidance and ensures compliance with laws.
  • Youth Leader: Represents youth interests and mobilizes young members.
  • Women Leader: Represents women's interests and mobilizes female members.
  • Director of Programs: Responsible for planning, coordinating, and supervising all programs, projects, and activities.
  • National Spokesperson: Serves as the official voice of the Association in all public communications.
  • Chief Security Officer: Responsible for the safety and security of members during meetings, rallies, and events.

3. TENURE OF OFFICE

All elected officers shall serve for a period of three (3) years, renewable once through democratic elections. No officer shall hold the same position for more than two consecutive terms.

4. ELECTION OF OFFICERS

Officers shall be elected at the General Assembly through secret ballot. Candidates must be financial members in good standing for at least one year before the election. Elections shall be supervised by an Electoral Committee appointed by the NEC.

5. SUCCESSION AND VACANCIES

In the event of resignation, death, or removal of an officer, the NEC shall appoint an acting officer until a by-election is conducted. The Vice President shall automatically assume the role of President in case of vacancy until elections are held.

6. ACCOUNTABILITY

All officers must present annual reports of their activities. Officers may be removed by a two-thirds majority vote of the General Assembly for misconduct, incompetence, or abuse of office.

ARTICLE 5

MEETINGS

1. TYPES OF MEETINGS

  • General Assembly: The supreme decision-making body of the Association, comprising all registered members.
  • Executive Committee Meetings: Held by the National Executive Committee (NEC) to manage day-to-day affairs.
  • State, Local Government, and Ward Meetings: Convened by respective chapters to coordinate grassroots activities.
  • Emergency Meetings: Called to address urgent matters requiring immediate attention.

2. FREQUENCY OF MEETINGS

  • General Assembly: Shall be held at least twice a year.
  • Executive Committee: Shall meet monthly or as determined by the President/Coordinator.
  • State/Local/Ward Chapters: Shall meet quarterly or as determined by their leadership.
  • Emergency Meetings: May be convened at any time by the President/Coordinator or upon written request by at least one-third of members.

3. NOTICE OF MEETINGS

Written or electronic notice of meetings shall be given at least 14 days before a General Assembly and 7 days before an Executive Committee meeting. Emergency meetings may be convened with at least 48 hours' notice.

4. QUORUM

A quorum for the General Assembly shall be one-third (1/3) of registered members. A quorum for Executive Committee meetings shall be two-thirds (2/3) of its members. No decisions shall be valid unless a quorum is met.

5. DECISION-MAKING

Decisions shall be made by simple majority vote of members present, except where otherwise stated in this Constitution. In case of a tie, the President/Coordinator shall have a casting vote.

6. CONDUCT OF MEETINGS

Meetings shall be conducted in an orderly manner, respecting democratic principles. Minutes of each meeting shall be recorded by the Secretary-General and circulated to members within 14 days.

ARTICLE 6

FINANCE

1. SOURCES OF FUNDS

  • Membership registration fees and annual dues.
  • Voluntary donations from members and supporters.
  • Fundraising activities such as rallies, lectures, and community events.
  • Grants or support from lawful organizations aligned with the Association objectives.
  • Any other legitimate source approved by the National Executive Committee (NEC).

2. FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

All funds shall be deposited into a recognized bank account in the name of the Association. The Treasurer and Financial Secretary shall jointly manage the account, with the President/Coordinator as a mandatory signatory. Withdrawals shall require at least two signatories, one of whom must be the President/Coordinator.

3. BUDGETING

The Financial Secretary shall prepare an annual budget, subject to approval by the NEC and ratification by the General Assembly. Expenditure shall be strictly in line with the approved budget.

4. FINANCIAL RECORDS

Accurate records of income and expenditure shall be maintained by the Treasurer. The Financial Secretary shall present quarterly financial reports to the NEC. Annual financial statements shall be presented to the General Assembly.

5. AUDIT

The accounts of the Association shall be audited annually by an independent auditor appointed by the NEC. Audit reports shall be circulated to all members and discussed at the General Assembly.

6. ACCOUNTABILITY AND SANCTIONS

Any officer found guilty of financial mismanagement or embezzlement shall be removed from office and may face legal action. Members have the right to demand transparency and accountability in financial matters.

ARTICLE 7

DISCIPLINE

1. CODE OF CONDUCT

All members shall uphold the values of democracy, unity, and respect for human rights. Members must conduct themselves in a manner that promotes the good image of the Association. Acts of violence, corruption, discrimination, hate speech, unlawful political activities or abuse of office are strictly prohibited.

2. OFFENCES

The following shall constitute misconduct: Gross insubordination to constituted authority; Financial mismanagement or embezzlement of Association funds; Engaging in activities that bring the Association into disrepute; Breach of confidentiality or unauthorized disclosure of Association information; Discrimination based on ethnicity, religion, gender, or political affiliation; Persistent absenteeism from meetings and activities without valid reason.

3. DISCIPLINARY COMMITTEE

The NEC shall establish a Disciplinary Committee to investigate allegations of misconduct. The Committee shall consist of at least five members, including the Legal Adviser, and shall operate independently. The Committee shall ensure fair hearing and due process in all cases.

4. DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURES

Complaints shall be submitted in writing to the Secretary-General, who shall forward them to the Disciplinary Committee. The accused member shall be notified in writing and given at least seven (7) days to respond. Hearings shall be conducted openly, and findings shall be reported to the NEC for action.

5. SANCTIONS

Depending on the gravity of the offence, sanctions may include: Verbal or written warning; Suspension from meetings and activities for a specified period; Removal from office (for elected officers); Expulsion from the Association; Referral to law enforcement agencies if the offence violates national laws.

6. APPEALS

A member sanctioned by the NEC may appeal to the General Assembly within 30 days. The decision of the General Assembly shall be final.

ARTICLE 8

AMENDMENTS

1. POWER TO AMEND

The Constitution of the Association may be amended by the General Assembly, which is the supreme authority of the Association. Amendments shall only be valid if carried out in accordance with the procedures outlined in this Article.

2. PROPOSAL OF AMENDMENTS

Any member in good standing may propose an amendment in writing to the Secretary-General. Proposed amendments must be supported by at least ten (10) members or one-third of the Executive Committee before being tabled for consideration.

3. NOTICE OF AMENDMENTS

The Secretary-General shall circulate proposed amendments to all members at least 30 days before the General Assembly where they will be debated. Copies of the proposed amendments shall be made available in both print and electronic formats for transparency.

4. DEBATE AND CONSIDERATION

Proposed amendments shall be debated openly at the General Assembly. Members shall have the right to speak for or against the amendment before voting. Amendments may be modified during debate, provided such modifications do not alter the fundamental intent.

5. APPROVAL OF AMENDMENTS

An amendment shall be adopted if approved by at least two-thirds (2/3) majority of members present and voting at the General Assembly. Once adopted, the amendment shall take immediate effect unless otherwise stated.

6. RECORD OF AMENDMENTS

All adopted amendments shall be incorporated into the Constitution by the Secretary-General. A revised version of the Constitution shall be circulated to members within 60 days of adoption.

ARTICLE 9

DISSOLUTION

1. POWER TO DISSOLVE

The Association may be dissolved only by a resolution passed at a General Assembly convened specifically for that purpose. Such resolution shall require the approval of at least two-thirds (2/3) of registered members present and voting.

2. NOTICE OF DISSOLUTION

Written notice of the proposed dissolution shall be circulated to all members at least 60 days before the General Assembly. The notice shall clearly state the reasons for dissolution and the proposed method of handling assets and liabilities.

3. PROCEDURE FOR DISSOLUTION

The General Assembly shall appoint a Dissolution Committee of not less than five (5) members to oversee the process. The Committee shall ensure that dissolution is carried out in a transparent, fair, and lawful manner.

4. ASSETS AND LIABILITIES

All outstanding debts and liabilities of the Association shall be settled before dissolution is finalized. Any remaining assets, funds, or properties shall not be distributed among members but shall be donated to: Charitable organizations, Civic education and democratic development initiatives, or Any other lawful cause approved by the General Assembly.

5. RECORDS AND DOCUMENTATION

The Secretary-General shall ensure that all records, minutes, and documents of the Association are properly archived. Copies of the dissolution resolution and final report shall be submitted to relevant authorities where applicable.

6. FINALITY OF DISSOLUTION

Once dissolution is completed and approved by the General Assembly, the Association shall cease to exist as a legal entity. No member shall have any claim to the assets or properties of the Association after dissolution.

ARTICLE 10

INTERPRETATION

1. DEFINITIONS OF TERMS

  • "Association" refers to TINUBU SOLIDARITY INITIATIVE.
  • "NEC" refers to the National Executive Committee of the Group.
  • "General Assembly" refers to the gathering of all registered members of the Association.
  • "Member in good standing" means a member who has fulfilled all obligations, including payment of dues, attendance at meetings, and adherence to the Constitution.
  • "Officer" refers to any elected or appointed leader of the Association at national, state, local, or ward level.
  • "Quorum" refers to the minimum number of members required to validate a meeting or decision as stipulated in this Constitution.
  • "Simple majority" means more than half of the members present and voting.
  • "Two-thirds majority" means at least two-thirds of the members present and voting.

2. AUTHORITY OF INTERPRETATION

The NEC shall have the authority to interpret the provisions of this Constitution in the first instance. Where ambiguity arises, the NEC's interpretation shall be subject to ratification by the General Assembly.

3. RESOLUTION OF AMBIGUITY

In cases of dispute over interpretation, the matter shall be referred to the General Assembly, whose decision shall be final and binding. If necessary, the Legal Adviser shall provide guidance to ensure interpretations are consistent with the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

4. CONSISTENCY WITH NATIONAL LAW

In the event of conflict between this Constitution and the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria or any applicable law with regards to interpretation, the national law shall prevail. The Association shall amend its Constitution accordingly to maintain compliance.

5. LANGUAGE OF THE CONSTITUTION

The official language of this Constitution shall be English. Translations into local languages may be provided for accessibility, but in case of conflict, the English version shall take precedence.

ARTICLE 11

SUPREMACY OF THE CONSTITUTION

1. BINDING AUTHORITY

This Constitution shall be the supreme guiding document of the Association. All members, officers, and organs of the Association are bound by its provisions and must act in accordance with them.

2. HIERARCHY OF RULES

Any rules, regulations, policies, or decisions made by the NEC, State Chapters, Local Government Chapters, or Ward Chapters must conform to this Constitution. Where there is a conflict between this Constitution and any subsidiary regulation, this Constitution shall prevail.

3. NULLIFICATION OF INCONSISTENT ACTS

Any action, decision, or regulation inconsistent with the provisions of this Constitution shall be deemed null and void. Members or officers who act contrary to the Constitution may face disciplinary measures as outlined in Article 7.

4. ALIGNMENT WITH NATIONAL LAW

This Constitution shall operate in harmony with the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria and all applicable laws. In the event of conflict between this Constitution and national law, the national law shall prevail, and the Association shall amend its Constitution accordingly.

5. PROTECTION AGAINST ARBITRARY CHANGE

No officer or organ of the Association shall unilaterally alter or suspend any provision of this Constitution. Amendments may only be made in accordance with the procedures set out in Article 8.

6. SUPREMACY CLAUSE

This Constitution shall remain the ultimate reference point for governance, discipline, finance, and operations of the Association. All members shall pledge loyalty to the Constitution upon admission into the Association.

ARTICLE 12

TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

1. INTERIM LEADERSHIP

Upon adoption of this Constitution, the founding members shall appoint interim officers to manage the affairs of the Association. Interim officers shall include at least a President/Coordinator, Secretary-General, Treasurer, and Public Relations Officer to ensure basic functionality.

2. DURATION OF INTERIM LEADERSHIP

Interim officers shall serve for a period not exceeding twelve (12) months from the date of adoption of this Constitution. Within this period, the NEC must organize the first General Assembly elections to establish permanent leadership.

3. FORMATION OF STRUCTURES

State, Local Government, and Ward Chapters shall be progressively established under the supervision of the NEC during the transitional period. Founding members shall provide guidance and oversight to ensure uniformity in structure and operations across all chapters.

4. DECISION-MAKING DURING TRANSITION

In the absence of elected officers, the founding members shall collectively make decisions on behalf of the Association. Such decisions shall be subject to ratification at the first General Assembly.

5. MEMBERSHIP ENROLMENT

During the transitional period, membership drives shall be conducted to register and mobilize citizens into the Association. All members enrolled during this period shall be recognized as pioneer members.

6. RESOLUTION OF TRANSITIONAL MATTERS

Any unresolved issues or disputes arising during the transitional period shall be decided by the founding members, subject to ratification at the first General Assembly. The transitional arrangements shall automatically lapse once the first elected NEC assumes office.

ARTICLE 13

SOURCES OF INCOME AND DISBURSEMENT OF FUNDS

1. SOURCES OF INCOME

The Association shall obtain its funds from donations, fund raising, grants, contributions, and other sources that the Trustees see fit.

2. APPLICATION OF FUNDS

The income and property of the Association shall be applied solely towards the promotion of the objectives of the Association, and no portion of it shall be transferred directly or indirectly by way of dividends, bonus, or by way of profit to any member of this Association. All monies received shall be deposited in the Association's designated account(s).

All budgets shall be approved by the Chairman, and the members of the Board of Trustees and expenditure of the Association shall be approved by the Chairman of the Board.

ARTICLE 14

COMMON SEAL AND INSTRUMENTS

1. COMMON SEAL

The Association shall have a common seal which shall be kept in the custody of the secretary, who shall produce it when required for use by the Trustees. All documents, contracts and instruments executed by and on behalf of the Association shall be signed and sealed with the common seal.

2. INSTRUMENTS

All instruments, contracts and documents shall be executed by the Chairman of the Board of Trustees and the Secretary or any other trustee that is specifically designated to execute such instrument by a resolution of the Board of Trustees.

ARTICLE 15

CONFLICT RESOLUTION

1. PRINCIPLES OF RESOLUTION

All conflicts within the Association shall be resolved in accordance with democratic principles, fairness, and respect for human dignity. Members are encouraged to seek peaceful dialogue and reconciliation before escalating disputes.

2. INTERNAL MECHANISMS

Disputes among members shall first be referred to the National Executive Committee (NEC) for mediation. If unresolved, the matter shall be referred to a Conflict Resolution Committee established by the General Assembly.

3. COMPOSITION OF THE CONFLICT RESOLUTION COMMITTEE

The Committee shall consist of at least five (5) impartial members, including the Legal Adviser. Members of the Committee must not have a direct interest in the dispute. The Committee shall serve for a renewable term of three (3) years and may be reconstituted as necessary.

4. PROCEDURES FOR RESOLUTION

Complaints must be submitted in writing to the Secretary-General, who shall forward them to the NEC or the Conflict Resolution Committee. The parties involved shall be given equal opportunity to present their case. Hearings shall be conducted in a transparent and impartial manner with records kept for accountability. The Committee shall issue a written decision within thirty (30) days of receiving the complaint.

5. LEVELS OF RESOLUTION

  • Mediation: Efforts shall first be made to reconcile parties through dialogue and compromise.
  • Arbitration: If mediation fails, the Committee shall arbitrate and issue binding recommendations.
  • General Assembly Decision: If arbitration fails, the matter shall be referred to the General Assembly, whose decision shall be final and binding.

6. EXHAUSTION OF INTERNAL REMEDIES

Members must exhaust all internal conflict resolution mechanisms before seeking external intervention. External intervention (e.g., legal action) shall only be pursued if the General Assembly authorizes it.

7. CONFIDENTIALITY

All proceedings of the Conflict Resolution Committee shall be confidential, except where disclosure is necessary for enforcement or compliance with national law.

ARTICLE 16

OATH OF OFFICE

1. MANDATORY OATH

All elected and appointed officers of the Association shall, before assuming office, take an oath of office before the General Assembly. The oath shall serve as a binding commitment to uphold the Constitution, act with integrity, and serve the Association faithfully.

2. ADMINISTRATION OF THE OATH

The oath shall be administered by the Legal Adviser or any officer designated by the NEC. It shall be taken publicly during a formal swearing-in ceremony to ensure transparency and accountability.

3. TEXT OF THE OATH

"I, [Name], having been elected/appointed as [Position], do solemnly swear to uphold and defend the Constitution of the TINUBU SOLIDARITY INITIATIVE, to serve with honesty, integrity, and accountability, to promote democracy, unity, and progress, and to discharge my duties faithfully without fear or favour. So help me God."

4. ALTERNATIVE AFFIRMATION

Members who prefer a secular affirmation may declare:

"I, [Name], having been elected/appointed as [Position], do solemnly affirm to uphold and defend the Constitution of the TINUBU SOLIDARITY INITIATIVE, to serve with honesty, integrity, and accountability, to promote democracy, unity, and progress, and to discharge my duties faithfully without fear or favour."

5. VALIDITY OF OFFICE

No officer shall assume the powers or duties of office until the oath has been duly taken. Refusal or failure to take the oath shall render the election or appointment invalid.

6. RECORD OF OATH

The Secretary-General shall record the oath-taking in the official minutes of the General Assembly. A signed copy of the oath shall be kept in the Association's archives for reference.

ARTICLE 17

MISCELLANEOUS

1. UNFORESEEN MATTERS

Any matter not expressly provided for in this Constitution shall be determined by the General Assembly in line with democratic principles and the objectives of the Association. The NEC may make temporary regulations to address unforeseen issues, but such regulations must be ratified at the next General Assembly.

2. SUPPLEMENTARY REGULATIONS

The NEC may issue guidelines, codes of conduct, or operational manuals to supplement this Constitution, provided they do not contradict its provisions. Such supplementary regulations shall be binding once approved by the General Assembly.

3. ACCESS TO THE CONSTITUTION

Copies of this Constitution shall be made available to all members in print and electronic formats. Ignorance of its provisions shall not be accepted as an excuse for misconduct or violation.

4. OFFICIAL LANGUAGE

The official language of this Constitution shall be English. Translations into local languages may be provided for accessibility, but in case of conflict, the English version shall prevail.

5. SEVERABILITY CLAUSE

If any provision of this Constitution is found to be invalid or unenforceable under Nigerian law, the remaining provisions shall continue to be valid and enforceable.

6. EFFECTIVE DATE

This Constitution shall take effect immediately upon adoption by the founding members/General Assembly and shall remain binding until lawfully amended or dissolved in accordance with its provisions.

ARTICLE 18

CLOSING DECLARATION

We, the undersigned founding members of TINUBU SOLIDARITY INITIATIVE having duly deliberated and agreed upon the provisions contained in this Constitution, do hereby adopt it as the supreme guiding document of the Association.

This Constitution shall take effect from the date of its adoption and shall remain binding on all members, officers, and organs of the Association until lawfully amended or dissolved in accordance with its provisions.

Adopted this 19th day of April, 2026, at Abuja, FCT, Nigeria
